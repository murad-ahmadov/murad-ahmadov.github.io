Murad Ahmadov

Independent researcher · Theoretical frameworks for mastery, evaluation, and epistemic limits

I am an independent theoretical researcher working on foundational frameworks for understanding mastery, learning, evaluation, and the limits of inference in human and artificial systems. My work is developed independently, without institutional affiliation or external funding.

Overview (for a general audience)

Much of modern education, testing, and AI evaluation assumes that correct performance reliably indicates understanding or competence. My work challenges this assumption.

Across a series of interrelated theoretical frameworks, I argue that mastery is a latent, structural property that cannot be guaranteed or fully inferred from surface behavior alone. This has implications for education, credentialing, AI benchmarking, and long-term knowledge stability.

Rather than proposing new tests or algorithms, these frameworks clarify what can and cannot be known in principle when we attempt to infer understanding from observable behavior.

Research and theoretical frameworks

My work forms a unified theoretical ecosystem centered on the Unified Mastery Theory (UMT) and its axiomatic foundation, the Unified Mastery Meta-Theory (UMMT).

Key components include:

  • Conceptual Mastery Evaluation Systems (CMES)
  • Mastery Equilibrium Theory (MET)
  • Functional Equivalence Class Hypothesis (FECH)
  • Counterfactual Predictive Sufficiency (CPS)
  • Reconstruction Lower Bounds for Learner Models (RLB-LM)
  • Taxonomy of Mastery Failure Modes (TMF)
  • Mastery Minimax Principle (MMP)
  • Construct Validity Framework (CVF)
  • Governance and lifecycle frameworks (MLI, EMS, REC)

These frameworks are modular but internally consistent and are intended to be read as parts of a single theoretical system.
